THE MATHEMATICS OF ROTA

Cyclic Group Theory (C₈), Topology & Circular Spatial Reasoning

1. The Cyclic Group C₈ & Rotational Invariance

Unlike square grid games like Tic-Tac-Toe where corners and edges have different geometric properties, every rim node in Rota belongs to the Cyclic Group C₈. A rotation by 45° ($g$) generates the entire group:

$$C_8 = \{ e, g, g^2, g^3, g^4, g^5, g^6, g^7 \}$$

Every position on the rim is structurally identical. Winning along the rim is governed by modular arithmetic: three stones at indices $(i, j, k)$ form a winning rim triplet if and only if:
$j \equiv (i + 1) \pmod 8$ and $k \equiv (i + 2) \pmod 8$.

2. The Diameter Antipodes (Spoke Lines)

Any line passing through the center connects two antipodal rim points separated by half the cycle ($180^\circ$):

$$\text{Antipode}(i) = (i + 4) \pmod 8$$

Together with the center hub ($C$), this forms the 4 diameter winning triplets: $(0, C, 4), (1, C, 5), (2, C, 6), (3, C, 7)$.

3. Circular Spatial Reasoning vs Grid Thinking

Traditional board games train Cartesian (X, Y) thinking. Rota trains polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$. Because the ends of the rim wrap around seamlessly ($7 \to 0$), players must train their brains to detect wrap-around threats like $(6, 7, 0)$ and $(7, 0, 1)$, preventing linear cognitive blind spots.

4. Sliding Topology & Deep Game Tree

With only 3 pieces per player and 9 sockets, there are 3 unoccupied sockets during the sliding phase. This graph ($C_8 + K_1$) creates non-trivial cycle transitions, making the movement phase dynamic and deep.

RULES OF ROTA

Ancient Roman Wheel Game (Terni Lapilli)

I

Drop Phase (Placing Pieces)

Each player begins with 3 stones. Players alternate placing one stone at a time onto any empty socket on the wheel.

II

Slide Phase (Movement)

Once all 6 stones are placed, players alternate selecting one of their stones and sliding it along a marked line (either around the rim or through a spoke) to an adjacent vacant socket. No jumping allowed.

III

Winning the Match

The first player to form 3-in-a-row wins! This can be either:
• Along any 3 adjacent sockets on the circular rim.
• Through the center along any of the 4 straight spoke diameters.

IV

Traps & Repetition

If a player is completely blocked with no legal moves on their turn, their opponent wins. If the exact board state repeats 3 times, the match is declared a draw (Pax Romana).
